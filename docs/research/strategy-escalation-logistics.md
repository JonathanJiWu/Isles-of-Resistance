# Research brief: strategic context, escalation and logistics

*Real-world analogue research (Taiwan Strait) for a fictional setting: Republic of Tessera ≈ Taiwan; Celestine Commonwealth ≈ PRC; Hesperian Federation ≈ United States. Compiled 19 Nov 2026 from open sources. Strategic, organizational and capability level only: no targeting, facility lists or operational advice. Labels: **[Fact]** documented/widely confirmed · **[Assessment]** expert or official estimate · **[Contested]** sources disagree · **[Speculative]** scenario, projection or wargame assumption. Short citations map to the numbered Sources list at the end.*

## A1. Interests, constraints and internal politics

### PRC (analogue for Celestine)
- [Fact] The 2005 Anti-Secession Law (Art. 8) requires "non-peaceful means and other necessary measures" if separatist forces "cause Taiwan's secession," if "major incidents entailing Taiwan's secession" occur, or if "possibilities for a peaceful reunification should be completely exhausted"; the State Council and Central Military Commission decide, then report to the NPC Standing Committee. The triggers are left undefined (ASL 2005).
- [Fact] Xi's standing formula: "strive for peaceful reunification" but "never promise to renounce the use of force" (CRS IF10275 2026).
- [Fact] Legal-symbolic pressure is growing: June 2024 judicial guidelines allow trials in absentia and the death penalty for "ringleader" "diehard separatists" (SPP Guidelines 2024); in Oct 2025 the NPC Standing Committee created a statutory 25 October "Taiwan Recovery Day" (NPC Decision 2025).
- [Assessment] DoD judges the PLA is progressing toward 2027 goals—China "expects to be able to fight and win a war on Taiwan by the end of 2027"—while leaders "remain unsure" they could seize Taiwan and counter US involvement at the same time (DoD CMPR 2025). Analysts call 2027 a capability "waypoint," not a decision date (Erickson/WOTR 2026; CRS IF12481 2026).
- [Assessment] Beijing runs a two-track strategy: threats against Taiwan's ruling DPP and inducements for the opposition, business and youth, plus propaganda casting Taiwan as an "abandoned chess piece" of the US (USCC 2025).
- [Contested] Sweeping PLA purges, including two top generals removed in Jan 2026, are read by DoD as short-term disruption that could yield a more disciplined force; some analysts link them to the 2026 lull in air activity (DoD CMPR 2025; Erickson/WOTR 2026; Defense News Mar 2026).

### Taiwan (analogue for Tessera)
- [Fact] NCCU identity series (data to Jun 2026): 63.1% "Taiwanese," 30.7% "both," 2.5% "Chinese" only, against 17.6/46.4/25.5 in 1992; roughly flat since 2020 (NCCU ESC 2026; Taipei Times Ed. Jul 2026).
- [Fact] About 6.4% favored eventual unification (Jun 2025), and 84.4% opposed "one country, two systems" in an April 2025 government-commissioned poll (USCC 2025).
- [Fact] Divided government: the KMT and TPP control the 113-seat legislature. Mass recall votes on 26 Jul and 23 Aug 2025 removed no KMT legislator, locking in division until the Jan 2028 national elections; local elections fall on 28 Nov 2026 (GTI Recall 2025; USCC 2025; CRS IF10275 2026).
- [Fact] Budget friction is a core constraint: the Cabinet's NT$1.25 trillion special defense budget was cut to NT$780bn (~US$25bn) on 8 May 2026, limited to US systems and stripped of domestic missile-defense and drone lines; the 2026 general budget passed only on 14 Aug 2026 after a record 266-day deadlock (Breaking Defense May 2026; Al Jazeera Aug 2026; CRS IF12481 2026).
- [Contested] Spending level: the 2026 proposal equalled 3.32% of GDP under a NATO-style definition (about 2.84% by the old method), and President Lai pledged 5% by 2030; critics call part of the rise reclassification (Focus Taiwan Aug 2025).
- [Fact] KMT chair Cheng Li-wun met Xi in Beijing on 10 Apr 2026, the first KMT chair–Xi meeting in about a decade, endorsing the "1992 Consensus" and opposing independence (NPR Jun 2026).

### United States (analogue for the Hesperian Federation)
- [Fact] Second Trump term: tariffs on Taiwan from Apr 2025 (32% announced, cut to 20% in Aug 2025); a record US$11.1bn arms notification on 17 Dec 2025; a ~US$14bn follow-on held "in abeyance" after the 14–15 May 2026 Beijing summit, where Trump said Lai "wants to go independent" (USCC 2025; CRS IF10275 2026; CRS IF12481 2026).
- [Fact] The Jan 2026 National Defense Strategy ranks homeland/Western Hemisphere defense first and "a strong denial defense along the First Island Chain" second, and never names Taiwan (CSIS NDS 2026).

### Japan
- [Fact] On 7 Nov 2025 PM Takaichi told the Diet a Taiwan contingency could be a "survival-threatening situation," the first sitting prime minister to say Japan might respond militarily. Beijing demanded retraction, issued a travel advisory, moved against Japanese seafood imports and postponed Japanese film releases (NBC Nov 2025).
- [Contested] Japanese opinion is split: 48.8% backed exercising collective self-defense in a Taiwan contingency and 44.2% opposed it (Kyodo Nov 2025). In Jul 2025 the defense minister had declined to discuss Japan's possible role in a Taiwan conflict (Defense News Jul 2025).

### Philippines, Australia, South Korea, EU
- [Assessment] Philippine interests center on South China Sea disputes but overlap with the Luzon Strait; after a 28 May 2026 Japan–Philippines move toward maritime-boundary talks, China Coast Guard formations began operating east of Taiwan (Asia Society 2026).
- [Assessment] Australia says it will not pre-commit forces, and Japan called a Pentagon request to clarify war roles "hypothetical" (Fox/FT Jul 2025). South Korea's President Lee said more "strategic flexibility" for US forces in Korea is "not an issue we can easily agree with" (KEIA 2025).
- [Fact] G7 foreign ministers, with the EU High Representative, criticized the April 2025 drills as destabilizing and in Nov 2025 opposed "unilateral attempts to change the status quo, particularly by force or coercion" (G7 Apr 2025; G7 Nov 2025).

## A2. Alliances and possible outside intervention
- [Fact] The Taiwan Relations Act (1979) commits the US to provide defense articles "in such quantity as may be necessary" and makes resisting force or coercion against Taiwan US policy, but does not say the US would defend Taiwan ("strategic ambiguity"); the 1982 Six Assurances complement it (CRS IF10275 2026).
- [Contested] Clarity versus ambiguity: Biden said four times that US forces would defend Taiwan (e.g., Sept 2022) while aides said policy was unchanged (BNN Bloomberg 2022). Clarity advocates say it deters; defenders say ambiguity restrains both Beijing and Taipei and pushes Taipei to invest in defense (CRS IF12481 2026).
- [Assessment] Intervention is uncertain even among experts: only 13% of surveyed US experts and 9% of Taiwan experts were "completely confident" the US would intervene militarily (CSIS Quarantine 2024).
- [Fact] Aid tools: the Taiwan Enhanced Resilience Act (FY2023) opened drawdown authority (three packages, US$1.5bn) and FMF (US$300m in FY2026); Congress funded a US$1bn Taiwan Security Cooperation Initiative for FY2026 (CRS IF12481 2026).
- [Fact] Japan's 2015 security laws allow limited collective self-defense only in a "survival-threatening situation" (an attack on a close partner posing "a clear danger of fundamentally overturning" people's rights), with prior Diet approval in principle. In an "important influence" situation the SDF may give non-combat rear support (supply, transport, medical) outside combat zones (Tokyo Foundation 2015).
- [Assessment] Japan is the swing ally: CSIS found US use of bases in Japan a necessary condition for defeating an invasion (CSIS First Battle 2023) and calls Japan "critical in nearly all" blockade scenarios and the preferred transshipment hub (CSIS Lights Out 2025).
- [Fact] EDCA grew from five to nine sites on 3 Apr 2023: three in northern Luzon (Cagayan, Isabela provinces) facing the Luzon Strait and one in Palawan. Manila calls them logistics and storage sites, not permanent US bases (ABS-CBN 2023). In Balikatan 2025 US Marines deployed NMESIS anti-ship launchers to the Batanes islands (AP Apr 2025).
- [Assessment] Economic deterrence: RAND finds sanctions alone unlikely to deter; Australia, Japan and the UK would probably join pre-emptive measures only under strong US leadership (RAND 2025).
- [Assessment] China lacks capable military partners while the US side gains combat power and strategic depth from allies, complicating Beijing's choice between keeping a war limited and striking US forces early (CNAS 2022).

## A3. Geography (capability level)
- [Fact] The strait is about 130 km wide at its narrowest point (AFP/Taipei Times 2022).
- [Contested] Amphibious weather windows: Easton cites April and October as the only "good suitability" months, with about six typhoons a year (ASPI Davis 2024); a US Naval War College report cited by AFP gives May–July and October (AFP/Taipei Times 2022). A December 2025 PLA drill weakened assumptions of seasonal predictability (Jamestown 2026).
- [Assessment] Landing sites are scarce: about 14 small suitable beaches (Easton via AFP/Taipei Times 2022) or "about a dozen" (CSIS First Battle 2023), mostly flanked by cliffs or dense urban terrain.
- [Fact] Outlying islands: Kinmen lies ~187 km from Taiwan and ~3–6 km from Fujian (pop. ~67,000); Matsu ~190 km from Taiwan and ~9–15 km from Fujian (~11,800); Penghu ~50 km from Taiwan (IDSA 2025).
- [Contested] Outlying-island seizure as a crisis path: many Western analysts treat it as plausible; IDSA rates a standalone seizure "very low" probability (IDSA 2025); CSIS warns it "might simply push Taipei farther away" (CSIS Lights Out 2025); the Dec 2025 drill reportedly rehearsed seizing Penghu (Jamestown 2026).
- [Fact] The first island chain runs from Japan through the Ryukyus, Taiwan and the Philippines; the Luzon Strait/Bashi Channel is the main deep-water gap south of Taiwan and now hosts allied anti-ship exercises (CSIS NDS 2026; AP Apr 2025).
- [Assessment] PLA strikes could reach 1,500–2,000 nautical miles from China, and over-the-horizon radars can probably track ships and aircraft between the first and second island chains (DoD CMPR 2025; Erickson/WOTR 2026).

## A4. Escalation pathways: what the record shows
- [Fact] PLA aircraft entering Taiwan's ADIZ: 972 (2021), ~1,733 (2022), ~1,703 (2023), 3,075 (2024), 3,764 (2025, +22.4%, a record) (DoD CMPR 2025; USCC 2025; CSIS ChinaPower 2026). DoD itself also gives 2,771 (to late Nov) and 3,067 for 2024, because counting windows differ.
- [Fact] Median line: Beijing denied in Sept 2020 that the line exists (CGTN 2020). Crossings rose from 22 (2020) to 1,472 (2024) (USCC 2025) and made up over 60% of PLA air activity around Taiwan in 2024 (DoD CMPR 2025).
- [Contested] Coast guard "law enforcement" around Kinmen: after a fatal Feb 2024 incident, DoD counts CCG entries into Kinmen/Matsu restricted waters at 13 per week in 2024 versus 8 in 2023 (DoD CMPR 2025), while Taiwan's coast guard counted ~60 incursion events in the following year (RFA 2025). The two probably count different things (individual ship entries vs. incursion events).
- [Fact] Named drills grew in scope: Aug 2022 (missiles over Taiwan); Joint Sword (Apr 2023); Joint Sword-2024A (May 2024, first coast guard integration, ~7 CCG ships); Joint Sword-2024B (Oct 2024, record 153 aircraft, first CCG encirclement) (DoD CMPR 2025); Strait Thunder-2025A (1–2 Apr 2025, simulated strikes on energy and port infrastructure) (GTI Strait Thunder 2025).
- [Fact] Justice Mission-2025 (29–30 Dec 2025) followed the US$11.1bn arms sale. It used 7–8 zones, several overlapping Taiwan's territorial sea, put rockets inside the 24 nm contiguous zone, disrupted 857 international flights and, for the first time, was framed as deterring outside intervention (TSM 2026; CSIS ChinaPower 2026; Reuters/bdnews24 Dec 2025). It was announced about a day ahead (Jamestown 2026).
- [Fact] The 2026 mix shifted from air to sea: from 1 Jan to 19 Aug, air sorties fell 51.1% (3,603 to 1,763) while "official ship" detections rose 149.7% (183 to 457), with CCG formations operating east of Taiwan from June; total observed activity fell about 30% (Asia Society 2026).
- [Contested] Why the air lull: restraint before Trump–Xi summits, the 2026 Iran war (jet fuel up more than 80%), the Two Sessions calendar, or PLA purges (Defense News Mar 2026).
- [Fact] In early Nov 2026 an unannounced, unnamed operation put over 100 PLAN and CCG vessels in the western Pacific, including four task forces from three theater commands, one of them a carrier group, operating between Taiwan and Guam. AEI-ISW assessed it might simulate isolating Taiwan on a larger scale than Justice Mission-2025; Beijing called the carrier activity routine training; Taiwanese officials called it "area denial" and "silent intimidation" (Taipei Times 8 Nov 2026; IBTimes/Reuters Nov 2026; Reuters/CGS Nov 2026). A Taiwanese analyst called it a "quiet exercise" that normalizes pressure without announcements (Taipei Times 4 Nov 2026).
- [Assessment] Warning time is shrinking: US and Taiwan officials warn the PLA could start a blockade within "a matter of hours" (USCC 2025).
- [Assessment] Quarantine is not blockade: CSIS defines a quarantine as a "law enforcement–led operation to control maritime or air traffic," framed as "enhanced customs inspection rules" with about 48 hours' notice and kept below armed conflict. A limited version might need only ~75% shipping compliance to be declared a success. Taiwan's coast guard (~10 ocean-going and ~160 small vessels) is dwarfed by the CCG (more than 150 and 400) (CSIS Quarantine 2024).
- [Assessment] DoD lists four PRC options: coercion short of war; a joint firepower strike; a joint blockade lasting "weeks or months," possibly with seizure of offshore islands; and a joint island landing campaign, the most decisive and riskiest (DoD CMPR 2025).
- [Assessment] Escalation logic: Beijing appears to be institutionalizing a pattern in which big drills punish specific US or Taiwanese acts while gray-zone pressure continues regardless (TSM 2026). In wargames both sides crossed red lines quickly, signals were misread, and China might brandish nuclear weapons to deter or end US involvement (CNAS 2022).

## A5. Major public wargames and their headline findings
- [Speculative] **CSIS, *The First Battle of the Next War* (Jan 2023; 24 runs; 2026 invasion).** The invasion usually failed if Taiwan resisted, the US entered at once, Japan allowed basing, and the US could mass long-range anti-ship strikes. Base-case losses: US ~270–283 aircraft and 17 ships (including 2 carriers) and ~3,200 killed in three weeks; Japan 112 aircraft and 26 ships; China ~155–161 aircraft, 138 major ships, roughly 10,000 killed and 30,000+ likely prisoners. Taiwan's navy was destroyed and the island left "without electricity and basic services." About 90% of allied aircraft losses happened on the ground, and the whole ~450-missile LRASM inventory was used in week one. With no US intervention ("Taiwan stands alone"), China won (CSIS First Battle 2023).
- [Speculative] **CSIS, *Lights Out?* (Jul 2025; 26 runs; 2028 blockade).** Nearly every scenario caused casualties, and blockades created escalation pressure that was hard to contain: 2 of 5 free-play games reached general war. Without US intervention, PRC submarines and mines sank 40% of inbound ships. Natural gas ran out in about 10 days, coal in about 7 weeks and oil in about 20 weeks; food was not the binding constraint; airlift and small blockade runners met only a small share of needs. Blockade was a poor precursor to invasion because it alerted others and cost China assets (CSIS Lights Out 2025).
- [Speculative] **CNAS, *Dangerous Straits* (Jun 2022; 2027).** No quick victory and a likely protracted war; neither side had the upper hand after the first week; escalation outran either capital's intent (CNAS 2022).
- [Assessment] Outcome drivers the studies name: Taiwanese will to resist; speed of US entry; Japanese basing; anti-ship missile stocks; whether the US strikes the mainland; air base hardening; and pre-war US forward posture, which created targets (CSIS First Battle 2023; CSIS Lights Out 2025).

## A6. Economic consequences
- [Contested] Chip concentration depends on the definition: TSMC makes more than 60% of contract chips and more than 90% of the most advanced (CSIS Lights Out 2025); Bloomberg assumes Taiwan makes ~62% of the most advanced logic chips (Bloomberg Feb 2026); TrendForce puts Taiwan at 66% of capacity at 16 nm and below in 2024, falling to 55% by 2027 as the US share rises from 10% to 22% (TrendForce 2024).
- [Assessment] About US$2.45 trillion of goods, over a fifth of global maritime trade, transited the strait in 2022 (CSIS Trade 2024).
- [Speculative] Bloomberg Economics (Feb 2026, first year): a war costs about US$10.6 trillion (9.6% of world GDP), with Taiwan −40%, China −11%, the US −6.6%, Japan −14.7% and South Korea −23%; a blockade costs the world −5.3%, Taiwan −12.5%, China −8.9% and the US −3.2% (Bloomberg Feb 2026). The Jan 2024 version put war at US$10T (10.2%) (Bloomberg Jan 2024), and Rhodium put a year-long blockade at about US$2T (CSIS Lights Out 2025).
- [Fact] Insurance tightened early: in 2023 Lloyd's asked syndicates to model Taiwan-conflict exposure, and insurers raised rates and added Taiwan exclusions (Reuters Aug 2023).
- [Assessment] Shipping lines would likely comply with Chinese notices to mariners; charters' "Five Powers War Risk Exclusion" ends cover in a war among the UN P5; commercial insurance breaks down at upper-single-digit attrition, forcing state-run, state-insured shipping (CSIS Lights Out 2025).
- [Fact] Tech-trade war: US controls on advanced chips and tools (7 Oct 2022, tightened 17 Oct 2023) (BIS 2023). China's Oct 2025 rare-earth controls were suspended for one year after the 30 Oct 2025 Trump–Xi meeting (Al Jazeera Oct 2025).
- [Fact] Coercion is selective: China levied tariffs on 134 Taiwanese products in May 2024 (DoD CMPR 2025) and used travel and seafood measures against Japan in Nov 2025 (NBC Nov 2025). Taiwan's exports to the US have overtaken those to China for the first time in more than two decades (USCC 2025).

## A7. Diplomacy, deterrence, public opinion and information operations
- [Fact] Diplomatic squeeze: Nauru switched recognition in Jan 2024 (DoD CMPR 2025), and countries endorsing Beijing's "One China principle" rose from 51 (Feb 2023) to 119 (Jan 2025) (USCC 2025).
- [Fact] US opinion (Jul 2025): 43% favor sending US troops if China invades (up from 36%), 47% favor using the Navy to break a blockade, 63% favor sending arms, 77% food and medical supplies, and 71% sanctions (Chicago Council 2025).
- [Fact] Taiwanese resolve: 63% were willing to resist "at all costs" (Mar 2025) (USCC 2025), and about 60% would resist even without US intervention, a figure that moved only about 2 points with US help assumed (Taipei Times Ed. Mar 2026).
- [Contested] Trust in the US: 37% thought the US likely to help in a war (Feb–Apr 2025, down from 45%) and positive views fell more than 20 points (USCC 2025), yet TPOF found 51.7% thought US intervention likely (TPOF 2025). Question wording drives the gap.
- [Fact] Cognitive and cyber pressure: about 2.2 million cyberattacks a day on government websites since 2024; 510,000 propaganda items logged by Apr 2025; narratives aimed at US reliability (Ukraine, tariffs, chips) (USCC 2025); proxy accounts amplified disinformation during the 2024 drills (DoD CMPR 2025).
- [Fact] Espionage prosecutions reached 64 in 2024 (versus ~11 a year in 2020–22); of the 159 people charged since 2020, about 60% were current or former military personnel (USCC 2025).
- [Contested] Countermeasures divide society: Lai's Mar 2025 designation of China as an "external adversarial force" drew 49% agreement and 43% disagreement, though most specific counter-infiltration measures polled well (USCC 2025).
- [Assessment] Deterrence signaling is now mixed: G7 statements oppose coercion (G7 Nov 2025), but after Justice Mission-2025 the State Department urged China to "cease military pressure" while Trump said he "wasn't worried" (TSM 2026).

## G1. Logistics and sustainability

### Energy and food
- [Fact] Taiwan's energy self-sufficiency is about 4.2%. Its 2025 power mix was 48% gas, 35% coal, 13% renewables and 1% nuclear. Reserves: LNG about 12 days, with a 14-day legal target by 2027; crude about 140 days; coal about 40 days against a 30-day minimum (CSIS Energy 2026).
- [Contested] Reserve figures vary by date and method: LNG is cited at 11–12 days with storage capacity for about 20, and coal at 42 days in early 2025; "days" are annual averages that rationing could stretch (USCC 2025; CSIS Energy 2026).
- [Fact] Taiwan's last reactor shut on 17 May 2025, and an Aug 2025 restart referendum failed its turnout threshold. In Mar 2026 Lai backed restarting two idled plants, with 2028 the earliest date (USCC 2025; CSIS Energy 2026).
- [Fact] The 2026 Iran war and Hormuz disruption forced about US$620m in extra spot LNG purchases; in 2024 about 34% of Taiwan's LNG and about 70% of its crude came from the Middle East (CSIS Energy 2026).
- [Fact] Calorie-based food self-sufficiency was 30.3% in 2023, the lowest since 2005 (Taipei Times Oct 2024).
- [Assessment] Even so, CSIS judged food non-binding in a blockade because of stocks, domestic output and modest shipping needs (CSIS Lights Out 2025).

### Undersea cables and communications
- [Fact] 24 undersea cables carry more than 99% of Taiwan's external traffic; there were at least 27 disruptions in 2019–2023, and a total cutoff is estimated to cost US$55.6m a day (USCC 2025).
- [Fact] In Feb 2023 both Matsu cables were cut, possibly accidentally, by two Chinese vessels, leaving about 14,000 residents with very slow internet for over 50 days (ASPI Rickards 2025). On 3 Jan 2025 a Chinese-owned, Cameroon-flagged ship damaged a cable off the north coast. In Feb 2025 the Togo-flagged Hong Tai 58 cut a Taiwan–Penghu cable, and its captain was jailed for three years on 12 Jun 2025 (USCC 2025; CNA Jun 2025). Taiwan now watches a blacklist of 52 "shadow fleet" ships (USCC 2025).
- [Fact] Backups are thin: OneWeb service via Chunghwa Telecom (2024) proved too small, a separate SES medium-orbit deal was signed, Amazon Kuiper/Leo talks are ongoing, and about 700 receivers are planned with priority for government, military, hospitals and finance (ASPI Rickards 2025). A Jul 2026 law change lets regulators waive foreign-ownership limits for satellite providers (Taiwan News Jul 2026).
- [Contested] Starlink: talks collapsed over Taiwan's demand for at least 50% local ownership and distrust of its owner's China ties; whether to admit it is still debated (ASPI Rickards 2025; Taiwan News Jul 2026).

### PLA lift
- [Assessment] DoD says the PLAN "almost certainly" lacks the conventional lift for a large-scale assault, is not significantly expanding its landing ships and craft, and trains with civilian roll-on/roll-off ships to fill the gap (DoD CMPR 2025).
- [Fact] "Shuiqiao" barges seen off Zhanjiang in Mar 2025 can link into a pier of about 820 m; the US Naval War College judged them vulnerable "from land, air and sea" (AFP/Taipei Times Apr 2025).
- [Assessment] China has fewer than 50 ro-ro ferries today and could have two to three times as many by 2032 (ASPI Davis 2024). Type 075/076 amphibious ships add helicopter- and drone-borne options (USCC 2025; Jamestown 2026).

### Distance, sealift, munitions and readiness (US/allied side)
- [Fact] US surge sealift rests on 45 Ready Reserve Force and 15 Military Sealift Command ships. The RRF averages 45 years old, with 17 ships at 50 or more; the US is short over 1,800 mariners for sealift beyond six months; the US-flag merchant fleet is 177 ships against China's 5,500-plus. TRANSCOM's commander named sealift and air refueling her "highest concerns" (House Select Cttee 2024).
- [Assessment] CSIS war games show some US long-range munitions running out "in less than one week" (CSIS Empty Bins 2023). Taiwan "must start the war with everything it needs," because there is no Ukraine-style overland resupply (CSIS First Battle 2023).
- [Fact] The 2026 Iran war (from 28 Feb) consumed about 1,700 Patriot, 200 THAAD and 150 Standard Missile interceptors; the CBO says replacing some will take at least five years; a US official described a "pause" in sales to Taiwan to preserve munitions, later called a "review" (CRS IN12668 2026; CRS IF12481 2026).
- [Fact] Availability is low: the F-35 mission-capable rate fell from 67% to 44% (full mission-capable from 38% to 25%) between FY2021 and FY2025 (GAO F-35 2026), and attack-submarine maintenance delays cost over 15,000 operational days in a decade (GAO SSN 2026).
- [Contested] Hardening versus dispersal: CSIS found about 90% of allied aircraft losses happened on the ground (CSIS First Battle 2023). Hudson's *Concrete Sky* counts hundreds of new Chinese aircraft shelters against a handful of US ones, noting a hardened base may need dozens of missiles to disable versus about six for an open one, while the USAF favors dispersal (Agile Combat Employment); Pacific Air Forces put about US$1bn of FY24 funds into hardening (AFM 2025).

### Civil defense, medical care and whole-of-society resilience
- [Fact] Taiwan's Whole-of-Society Defense Resilience Committee (2024) works on five pillars: civilian training, stockpiles, infrastructure, medical care and evacuation, and communications and finance. Its target is 400,000 trained citizens (DoD CMPR 2025).
- [Fact] Han Kuang 41 (9–18 Jul 2025) ran ten days with about 22,000 reservists, alongside an "Urban Resilience" exercise that merged air-raid and civil-defense drills (AP Jul 2025).
- [Fact] The "In Case of Crisis" public safety guide (Sept 2025) went to about 9.8 million households; it tells citizens that any claim of surrender is false (Taipei Times Sep 2025).
- [Assessment] Critics still call civil defense insufficient and energy, water, communications and other infrastructure vulnerable (CRS IF12481 2026).
- [Contested] Drone gap: Taiwan reportedly has fewer than 5,000 military drones while China reportedly ordered close to a million (Al Jazeera Aug 2026).

## Plausible campaign phases
*Alternative pathways abstracted from the patterns above and recast in setting terms (Mainland = Celestine, Island = Tessera, Ally = Hesperian Federation). They are not one sequence: a campaign can open in any of them, move between them, or end in one. All are [Speculative].*

**P1. The Long Squeeze (gray-zone normalization)** [Speculative]
- *Readiness:* the Island stays on elevated peacetime alert, which costs money and morale over time; the Mainland trains its forces under near-real conditions.
- *Surveillance:* constant tracking of routine sorties and patrols; the signal is hard to read because the activity is normal.
- *Sea/air tension:* daily air-zone entries, a fading median line, coast guard patrols at the frontier islands; the mix can shift from air to sea.
- *Infrastructure/trade:* occasional deniable cable cuts and slow tightening of insurance.
- *Outside involvement:* statements, transits and arms sales.
- *Negotiation:* frozen at the top, while the Mainland courts the Island's opposition.
- *Exits:* a political trigger leads to P2; summitry to P7; normalized coast guard jurisdiction to P3.

**P2. Punitive Thunder (a named exercise after a trigger)** [Speculative]
- *Readiness:* the Island surges for 2–4 days.
- *Surveillance:* about one day of warning, and the drill looks like mobilization.
- *Sea/air tension:* record sorties, encirclement, live-fire zones next to territorial waters, coast guard "inspections."
- *Infrastructure/trade:* flights cancelled, ships rerouted, short insurance spikes.
- *Outside involvement:* G7-type statements, though the Ally's leader may play the event down.
- *Negotiation:* none during the drill; the crisis usually ends in days.
- *Exits:* back to P1, or an unannounced continuation into P3 or P5.

**P3. Customs Cordon (law-enforcement quarantine)** [Speculative]
- *Readiness:* the Island's coast guard is outmatched, and committing the navy risks escalation.
- *Surveillance:* the focus shifts to boardings, seizures and whether merchant ships comply.
- *Sea/air tension:* "inspection rules" with 48 hours' notice, then boardings with no shots fired; the Mainland can claim success at partial compliance.
- *Infrastructure/trade:* shipping lines comply, insurers withdraw, and the Island's energy clocks start.
- *Outside involvement:* legal ambiguity, debate among allies over escorts versus sanctions.
- *Negotiation:* the Mainland offers to lift the cordon for concessions; face-saving off-ramps decide the outcome.
- *Exits:* coerced accommodation (P7), escorted convoys (P5), or the cordon fizzles (P1).

**P4. Frontier Islands Squeeze** [Speculative]
- *Readiness:* small garrisons with symbolic weight and little military value.
- *Surveillance:* mostly local.
- *Sea/air tension:* jurisdiction claims, ferry and cable disruption, possibly a seizure.
- *Infrastructure/trade:* supply and communications cuts to small populations.
- *Outside involvement:* the Ally's commitment to tiny islets is unclear.
- *Negotiation:* the islets become bargaining chips.
- *Exits:* a seizure may galvanize the Island and its allies and backfire (P5), or the dispute quietly resolves (P1).

**P5. Iron Ring (military blockade with an escalation ladder)** [Speculative]
- *Readiness:* full mobilization, civil defense and rationing.
- *Surveillance:* anti-submarine and mine detection, convoy routing.
- *Sea/air tension:* the Mainland climbs from submarines and mines to open attacks at sea to wider war, while the Island and Ally climb from constrained to assertive defense to direct combat.
- *Infrastructure/trade:* without resupply, gas runs out in about 10 days, coal in about 7 weeks and oil in about 20 weeks; the world economy takes a roughly 5% hit.
- *Outside involvement:* the Ally must accept the Island's capitulation or intervene; transshipment through allied ports; sanctions.
- *Negotiation:* signaling to de-escalate, and off-ramps that let the Mainland declare victory.
- *Exits:* the blockade is broken (P7), the Island capitulates, or general war (P6).

**P6. Firestorm and Landing (strikes, then invasion)** [Speculative]
- *Readiness:* total, and the Island must hold the first weeks alone with what it already has.
- *Surveillance:* contested, with attacks in space and cyberspace.
- *Sea/air tension:* opening strikes catch aircraft on the ground; amphibious and air assault follow; the Mainland strikes regional bases to keep the Ally out.
- *Infrastructure/trade:* the Island loses power, and the world economy takes a roughly 10% shock.
- *Outside involvement:* allied basing decides the outcome; key munitions run out in about a week.
- *Negotiation:* war termination under a nuclear shadow.
- *Exits:* a pyrrhic defeat of the landing, a stalemate around captured ports, or Mainland victory if the Island stands alone.

**P7. Cold Peace (transactional détente)** [Speculative]
- *Readiness:* the Island's budgets stall in its legislature, the Ally pauses arms deliveries, and trust in the Ally falls.
- *Surveillance:* fewer sorties but more coast guard ships, as pressure moves into "law enforcement."
- *Sea/air tension:* air pressure falls by about half while maritime jurisdiction quietly expands.
- *Infrastructure/trade:* trade truces, and resource export curbs paused but able to return.
- *Outside involvement:* allies hedge.
- *Negotiation:* summits and the Mainland's contacts with the Island's opposition; the Island fears being bargained over.
- *Exits:* a failed summit leads to P2; a sudden unannounced mass deployment to P3 or P5.

## Design implications
1. **Separate energy clocks.** Track gas (~10–12 days, about half of power generation), coal (~6–7 weeks) and oil (~20 weeks) separately. Rationing stretches the clocks at a cost to economy and morale, and convoys or airlift refill only a fraction. (CSIS Lights Out 2025; CSIS Energy 2026)
2. **Ambiguous warning.** Exercises look like mobilization, notice is about a day, and some operations are never announced or come out of season. Give players an intelligence-confidence meter where false alarms (cancelled flights, insurance spikes) and surprises both carry costs. (Jamestown 2026; Taipei Times 8 Nov 2026; USCC 2025)
3. **A legal-threshold ladder.** Coast guard "law enforcement" gives the Mainland cheap moves; answering coast guards with warships raises escalation and political cost, and the Ally hesitates below the armed-conflict line. (CSIS Quarantine 2024)
4. **Shipping compliance and insurance.** Commercial shipping obeys notices and insurers exit at low attrition, so the Island needs state charters and insurance pools, requisition of Island-owned merchant ships and a reserve of LNG tankers. (CSIS Lights Out 2025; Reuters Aug 2023)
5. **A mirrored escalation matrix.** Each side picks an escalation level each turn, with spiral risk and "declare victory" off-ramp cards. (CSIS Lights Out 2025; CNAS 2022)
6. **Basing-access gate.** The Ally's air power depends on a neighbor's legal threshold—a "survival-threatening" ruling allows combat, "important influence" only rear support—and that neighbor's public is split roughly 50/50. (Tokyo Foundation 2015; Kyodo Nov 2025; CSIS First Battle 2023)
7. **Aircraft are lost on the ground.** Investments in shelters, dispersal and runway repair raise the number of missiles needed per base. (CSIS First Battle 2023; AFM 2025)
8. **Finite munitions.** Stocks replenish over years, crises in other theaters drain the Ally's stockpile, and arms deliveries can pause for diplomacy. (CSIS Empty Bins 2023; CRS IN12668 2026; CRS IF12481 2026)
9. **Distance as a throughput cap.** An aging sealift fleet, mariner shortages and limited tankers cap Ally deliveries, which route through transshipment hubs. (House Select Cttee 2024; CSIS Lights Out 2025)
10. **Unit availability.** Units carry readiness percentages (for example, 44% mission-capable) and sit in maintenance queues. (GAO F-35 2026; GAO SSN 2026)
11. **Fragile communications.** Cables carry almost all traffic and can be cut deniably, repairs take weeks, and satellite backup serves priority users only. (USCC 2025; ASPI Rickards 2025)
12. **Island politics meter.** A divided legislature can cut or delay budgets (a 38% cut, a 266-day delay), and opposition talks with the Mainland and local elections move the meter; national identity and willingness to resist set a morale floor. (Breaking Defense May 2026; Al Jazeera Aug 2026; NCCU ESC 2026; Taipei Times Ed. Mar 2026)
13. **Ally credibility meter.** Arms held for summits, tariffs and "abandoned chess piece" propaganda erode the Island's trust; the Ally's public backs aid more than combat (43% for troops). (USCC 2025; Chicago Council 2025; CRS IF10275 2026)
14. **Seasonal sea state.** Weather windows change landing odds but not drill timing. (AFP/Taipei Times 2022; ASPI Davis 2024; Jamestown 2026)
15. **Shared economic pain.** War or blockade shocks every economy, and sanctions are one tool among several, not a deterrent on their own; the Mainland can punish third parties through travel and trade. (Bloomberg Feb 2026; RAND 2025; NBC Nov 2025)
