# Research brief: air forces, air and missile defense, drones

Scope: open-source, capability/role level only (no targeting, tactics, exploits or real facilities as targets). Real-world systems are cited only as inspiration for fictional rosters. Research cut-off: Nov 2026.

Labels (one per bullet): **[Fact]** officially documented or widely confirmed · **[Assessment]** expert/official estimate or analysis · **[Contested]** sources disagree (how is stated) · **[Speculative]** projection or scenario assumption. Short cites refer to the numbered Sources list.

## B. Air forces

### B1. PLA aviation: scale and mix
- [Assessment] PLAAF + PLAN Aviation are the largest aviation force in the Indo-Pacific and third largest in the world: over 3,150 aircraft (excluding trainers and UAVs), ~2,400 of them combat aircraft (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]).
- [Assessment] More than 1,300 of ~1,900 PLA fighters are fourth-generation; over 225 J-16 multirole fighters were in service in 2023 (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]).
- [Assessment] PLA exercises around Taiwan showed "a large number of advanced aircraft capable of conducting operations against Taiwan without requiring refueling" (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]).
- [Assessment] DoD lists the J-16 armed with the PL-17 very-long-range air-to-air missile against "HVAA" (high-value airborne assets such as AEW and tankers), with ~1,400 km total reach (fighter radius plus missile) (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]).
- [Contested] J-20 stealth fighter numbers: IISS counted "at least 150" in early 2023; RUSI estimated ~300 in at least 13 regiments by late 2025, with ~120/yr production; an independent analyst believes ~500 delivered by mid-2026. All rely on serial-number and imagery methods (TWZ 2026 [6]).
- [Fact] The land-based J-35A was unveiled at Zhuhai in Nov 2024, and China showed two tailless stealth aircraft designs the same year (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]). The naval J-35 was displayed on the carrier Fujian at its commissioning (Aviationist 2025 [7]).

### B2. Taiwan, US and Japan fighters and interceptors
- [Fact] Taiwan upgraded 141 F-16A/Bs to F-16V (completed 2023). The first 2 of 66 new F-16 Block 70s arrived on Oct 2, 2026, after years of slippage (production-line, supply-chain and EW-suite delays). A ROCAF historian counts ~53 Mirage 2000s and ~129 F-CK-1s (TWZ Oct 2026 [9]; TSM 2025 [29]).
- [Assessment] Taiwan's AMRAAM stock is estimated at roughly 300–400 missiles, about two per F-16 (TWZ 2024 [23]).
- [Fact] Japan's ASDF flew 704 scrambles in FY2024 (464, or 66%, against Chinese aircraft) and 595 in FY2025 (~61% Chinese). In Dec 2025 carrier-launched J-15s illuminated JASDF F-15s with radar (Japan JSO 2025 [16]; 2026 [17]).
- [Assessment] A USAF general projected ~12:1 Chinese numerical superiority in modern fighters west of the dateline by 2027 (5:3 in fifth-generation) (TWZ 2026 [6]).

### B3. Electronic-warfare aircraft
- [Assessment] The Y-9 communications-jamming aircraft (GX-11) can disrupt adversary battlespace awareness at long range (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]).
- [Fact] The J-16D EW fighter was first seen in Taiwan's ADIZ in Jan 2022. It is reported to carry jamming and EW pods. A Taiwanese analyst said it could suppress radars and open the way for follow-on strikes (Defense Post/AFP 2022 [22]).
- [Fact] The J-15D carrier EW jet debuted at Zhuhai 2024 (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]). The J-15DT is listed for Fujian's air wing (Aviationist 2025 [7]).

### B4. Airborne early warning & control (AEW&C)
- [Assessment] KJ-500 production continued "at a rapid pace" alongside the KJ-2000 and KJ-200. AEW extends PLA radar coverage "well past" ground radars, and at least one KJ-500 has a refuelling probe (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]).
- [Fact] China revealed a Y-20B-based AEW&C program meant to track stealth aircraft (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]), and the carrier-based KJ-600 now flies from Fujian (Aviationist 2025 [7]).
- [Assessment] Taiwan has five operational E-2Ks and has asked twice to buy six E-2Ds. Washington reportedly rejected the first request as redundant with ground radar (Taipei Times Feb 2025 [10]).
- [Contested] The US E-7 Wedgetail was zeroed in the FY2026 request (Hegseth called it "not survivable"), restored by Congress, zeroed again for FY2027, then backed by a May 2026 budget amendment. Its long-term future is unresolved (Breaking Defense May 2026 [11]).

### B5. Tankers and transports
- [Assessment] The PLA had 51 Y-20A heavy transports and 16 Y-20U tankers as of March 2024, plus "a small number" of IL-78s. Y-20Us have supported over-water flights into Taiwan's ADIZ and the Philippine Sea (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]).
- [Assessment] In CSIS wargames, PLA tanker shortages made distant air patrols (east of Taiwan) "unwise", so PLA teams concentrated air power over Taiwan itself (CSIS 2023 [3]).
- [Assessment] US tankers depend on a few large runways. Losing tanker runways could impair operations for over a month, and without refuelling, short-range fighters cannot carry most weapons into range (Breaking Defense on Stimson 2024 [13]).
- [Assessment] A 2026 Hudson study says the KC-46 "lacks the range, offload and survivability" for a China fight. Tankers are high-priority targets, yet hardened shelters have mostly been built for fighters (Breaking Defense on Hudson 2026 [15]).

### B6. Bombers as standoff missile carriers
- [Assessment] The H-6K carries six land-attack cruise missiles or six YJ-12 anti-ship missiles, and can reach the Second Island Chain from home airfields. The H-6N is air-refuellable and carries an air-launched ballistic missile (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]).
- [Assessment] DoD gives H-6 total strike reach as 3,100–4,000 km with anti-ship missiles and 4,000–5,500 km with YJ-21/CJ-20 (bomber radius ~3,500 km). A bomber-launched YJ-21 hypersonic anti-ship missile was revealed in May 2024 (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]).
- [Speculative] The H-20 stealth bomber "may debut sometime in the next decade" with >10,000 km range (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]). No public unveiling was found in the sources reviewed.
- [Assessment] CSIS found bombers firing standoff missiles from beyond SAM range among the hardest assets to negate, and recommended prioritising bombers over fighters (CSIS 2023 [3]).

### B7. Maritime patrol, ASW and helicopters
- [Assessment] The PLAN flies a Y-9 ASW/maritime-patrol variant with a magnetic-anomaly boom and surface-search radar. The Z-20F is replacing smaller ship-based ASW helicopters (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]).
- [Assessment] PLA Army Aviation trains for over-water, ultra-low-altitude flight and for operating from Navy and civilian ships. Three Z-8 battalions can lift a combat battalion at once, and the Z-20 serves special-forces insertion and shipborne ASW (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]).
- [Fact] The Z-10 attack helicopter was first shown abroad in Feb 2024 (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]).
- [Assessment] The Dec 2025 "Justice Mission" drill rehearsed helicopter-borne air assault with a Type 075 amphibious-assault-ship task force. Taiwan's MND counted ~210 aircraft sorties over the drill (Jamestown 2026 [21]).
- [Fact] Japan reported a PLA Y-9 intelligence aircraft entering Japanese airspace for the first time in FY2024 (Japan JSO 2025 [16]).

### B8. Basing, shelters, sortie generation, runway repair
- [Assessment] In CSIS's 24-iteration wargame, about 90% of US, Japanese and Taiwanese aircraft losses occurred on the ground. The US lost 168–372 aircraft in base scenarios, and Taiwan lost roughly half its air force, mostly to missile strikes (CSIS 2023 [3]).
- [Assessment] CSIS counted ~250 hardened aircraft shelters (HAS) in Taiwan and judged its underground hangar facilities probably survivable. It also found the PLA's short-range missile volume large enough to threaten every Taiwanese air base, and surviving aircraft hard to fuel, arm and maintain (CSIS 2023 [3]).
- [Assessment] Hudson's "Concrete Sky" counts over 3,000 PLA aircraft shelters. Within 1,000 nm of the Strait the PLA has 134 air bases with >650 HAS and ~2,000 lighter shelters, plus 20 new runways and >40 runway-length taxiways. The US added 2 HAS and 41 lighter shelters in the region since the early 2010s (TWZ on Hudson 2025 [12]).
- [Assessment] Stimson modelled initial missile strikes closing allied air bases for up to 12 days. It judged that no mix of runway repair, dispersal and missile defense solves the problem, and recommended long-range drones and missiles that do not depend on runways (Breaking Defense on Stimson 2024 [13]).
- [Assessment] Stimson's 2026 "Runway Trap" argues that Taiwan's few airfields and limited rapid runway repair could ground its air force for days or weeks. It urges an "air denial" approach built on mobile SAMs, EW and runway-independent drones (Stimson 2026 [14]; page blocked, summary from search listing).
- [Assessment] CSIS advised "do not plan on overflying the Chinese mainland" because Chinese air defense is too strong (CSIS 2023 [3]).

## D. Integrated air and missile defense

### D1. The PLA missile threat by category
- [Assessment] DoD's 2024 table gives PLARF inventories of 300 launchers / 900 missiles for SRBMs (300–1,000 km), 300 / 1,300 for MRBMs (1,000–3,000 km), 250 / 500 for IRBMs (3,000–5,500 km) and 150 / 400 for ground-launched cruise missiles (>1,500 km) (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]).
- [Fact] The 2025 report lists fielded conventional systems by class and range but publishes no updated launcher or missile counts. Systems listed: SRBM DF-11A/15/16; MRBM DF-17/21; GLCM CJ-10/100; IRBM DF-26; DF-27, the last three with land-attack and anti-ship roles (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]).
- [Assessment] The DF-17 hypersonic-glide-vehicle (HGV) missile is deployed, and its HGV is "designed to evade adversary radars and BMDs". DoD calls China's hypersonic arsenal the world's leading one (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]; 2025 [2]).
- [Assessment] DoD says the PLARF is prepared to strike high-value targets such as command posts, air bases and radar sites to degrade Taiwan's defenses. In Oct 2024 a PLARF drone unit flew ISR for a Taiwan exercise for the first time (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]).

### D2. Taiwan's layered defenses
- [Assessment] Sky Bow III (Tien Kung III) and PAC-3 are expected to remain the backbone of Taiwan's air defense for 3–5 years. Taiwan can surge-produce 96 Tien Kung III missiles a year. The Strong Bow (Chiang Kung) upper-tier interceptor and Tien Kung IV will not enter service before 2028 at current funding (Taipei Times Oct 2026 [24]).
- [Fact] Taiwan bought 102 PAC-3 MSE interceptors (~NT$20B), with the first batch delivered in Jan 2026, and formed a fourth Patriot battalion in 2025 (Defense Post Feb 2026 [25]).
- [Fact] The US approved three NASAMS fire units, Sentinel radars and up to 123 AMRAAM-ER missiles (~$1.16B) in Oct 2024. NASAMS is a medium-range system suited to cruise missiles and one-way attack drones, with very limited capability against short-range ballistic missiles (TWZ 2024 [23]).
- [Contested] NASAMS delivery timing: a production contract followed in 2025, but Taipei Times reported delivery delays in Oct 2026 (TSM 2025 [29]; Taipei Times Oct 2026 [24]).
- [Fact] 500 Stingers were due by end-2025 and the MND requested ~2,000 more. Taiwan's arms-sale backlog stood at ~$21.5B in Oct 2025 (TSM 2025 [29]). CSIS put it at ~$32B in May 2026, including NASAMS, PAC-3 MSE and Altius drones (CSIS 2026 [4]).
- [Fact] President Lai announced "T-Dome" on Oct 10, 2025, promising "multi-layered defence, high-level detection, and effective interception". It is a networked integration of Patriot, Sky Bow and Strong Bow (Reuters 2025 [26]; Defense Post 2026 [25]).
- [Fact] On May 8, 2026 the opposition-controlled legislature cut the proposed NT$1.25T special defense budget to NT$780B, limited to US arms purchases. Domestic procurement, including Taiwan-made drones, was excluded (Defense Post/AFP 2026 [27]; Taipei Times May 2026 [28]).

### D3. Magazine depth and cost-exchange
- [Fact] The US Navy spent close to $1B on munitions in about six months of Red Sea and Middle East operations (Stars and Stripes 2024 [31]). The Eisenhower carrier group alone expended 155 Standard missiles, 135 Tomahawks, 60 air-to-air missiles and 420 air-to-surface munitions (TWZ 2024 [32]).
- [Assessment] Estimated unit costs are ~$2.5M for an SM-2 and ~$4.3M for an SM-6 (TWZ 2024 [32]). The Navy cannot rearm ships at sea (TWZ 2024 [32]).
- [Assessment] A Patriot round costs over $3M and a NASAMS round about $1M, against ~$35k for a Shahed (CSIS estimate) and $3–5k for an interceptor drone. Using a Patriot on a drone is roughly an 85:1 cost exchange (Defense News Mar 2026 [33]).
- [Contested] After the 2026 Iran war, CSIS estimates that ~65% of US Patriot interceptors were expended (fewer than 1,000 left) and 234–278 THAAD remain. The Pentagon has not confirmed these figures (Defense News Jul 2026 [30]).
- [Assessment] Rebuilding US interceptor stocks will take years: more than 2 years for PAC-3 MSE and about 3 years for THAAD and SM-6. In CSIS wargames, some long-range US missile stocks ran out within the first week of a Taiwan conflict (CSIS 2026 [4]).
- [Assessment] Ukraine intercepted ~84% of Russian missiles and drones from 2022 to 2024. Faster and ballistic missiles were harder to stop, and only very large salvos (>150) clearly reduced interception rates (CSIS Futures Lab 2025 [5]).
- [Assessment] Stimson's model had US Patriot/THAAD inventories exhausted within the first day of a regional war (Breaking Defense on Stimson 2024 [13]).

### D4. Sensors and the PLA's own air defense
- [Assessment] The PLA's IADS is "robust and redundant" over land and within 300 nm of its coast, built on HQ-9/9B, S-300 and S-400 SAMs, fighters and an extensive early-warning radar network (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]).
- [Assessment] PLA skywave over-the-horizon radars can probably detect ships and aircraft between the first and second island chains, and new counter-stealth radars have been built on island outposts (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]).

### D5. Passive defenses
- [Assessment] Analysts emphasise hardening (HAS), redundancy, prepositioned supplies and camouflage, concealment and deception. Lighter shelters protect less but hide aircraft counts (TWZ on Hudson 2025 [12]). Dispersal and hardening "dilute the effects of missile attacks" (CSIS 2023 [3]).
- [Assessment] Hudson recommends hardened shelters for tankers and distributed fuel storage (Breaking Defense on Hudson 2026 [15]).
- [Fact] Taiwan created a Whole-of-Society Defense Resilience Committee in 2024, with a goal of training 400,000 citizens (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]).

### D6. Counter-drone methods
- [Assessment] Electronic warfare dominates counter-drone fighting. RUSI estimated Ukraine was losing ~10,000 drones a month in 2023, largely to Russian EW, with at least one major EW system per 10 km of front (RUSI 2023 [35]).
- [Assessment] The PLA's counter-drone mix combines self-propelled gun/missile vehicles (e.g., a 25 mm six-barrel cannon with four FN-16 MANPADS), small EW systems and MANPADS (DoD CMPR 2024 [1]).
- [Assessment] Interceptor drones now down about one in three Russian aerial targets over Ukraine, and >70% of Shahed kills over Kyiv in Feb 2026. Ukraine produced ~100,000 of them in 2025. They need radar cueing, depend on operators, and struggle against faster jet-powered drones and in cold weather (Defense News Mar 2026 [33]).
- [Fact] A Taiwanese contract for 26 fixed-site counter-drone systems failed acceptance tests and is being annulled (Taipei Times Jul 2026 [40]).
- [Assessment] Taiwan's counter-drone systems are not yet linked to a C2 system, integration is targeted for end-2027, and uncoordinated jammers risk disabling friendly drones (Focus Taiwan Aug 2026 [41]).
- [Fact] US counter-small-drone efforts (Replicator 2) were consolidated into Joint Interagency Task Force 401 in Aug 2025 (CRS 2025 [50]).
- [Assessment] **Lasers:** CRS says high-energy lasers offer low cost per shot (the cost of electricity) and deep magazines given enough power, but "rain, fog, obscurants" can limit range and beam quality (CRS 2024 [36]).
- [Assessment] The US Army's 50 kW Stryker lasers drew poor soldier feedback in 2024 over heat, bulk and dust degrading the beam, though 20 kW systems worked at some fixed sites (Breaking Defense 2024 [38]).
- [Fact] Israel's Iron Beam was declared operational in Sept 2025 and is meant to supplement missile interceptors against small threats. Its main downside is that it "does not function well in low visibility" or heavy cloud (Times of Israel 2025 [37]).

### D7. EW and countermeasures in air defense
- [Assessment] Decoys are a saturation tool. About 40% of Russia's ~5,100–5,400 monthly Shahed-type launches in late 2025 were decoys, used to distract and exhaust air defenses and for reconnaissance. Mass raids carried a lower share of real strike drones (ISIS Jan 2026 [34]).
- [Assessment] Russian Shahed hit rates rose from 2–3% in early 2025 to ~17% by Dec 2025. Raids of 100–200 drones a day reached 40–50% effectiveness, so saturation raises leak-through (ISIS Jan 2026 [34]).
- [Fact] The US MALD decoy mimics allied aircraft signatures to confuse an IADS. MALD-J adds stand-in jamming, with up to 575 miles range and 90 minutes endurance, and is launched from F-16s and B-52s (Air & Space Forces [39]).

## E. Drones and unmanned systems

### E1. PRC
- [Assessment] The WZ-7 jet ISR drone has a joined wing, an estimated ceiling above 60,000 ft and endurance of 10+ hours, with possible SIGINT/EW roles. It made its first Sea of Japan flight in Mar 2024 and has been active around the Taiwan Strait (TWZ 2024 [19]).
- [Assessment] BZK-005 and TB-001 MALE drones likely flew reconnaissance on joint patrols circling Taiwan in 2024 (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]). Japan scrambled fighters against a TB-001 in Feb 2025 (Japan JSO 2025 [18]) and first identified WL-10 and GJ-2 armed drones in FY2024 (Japan JSO 2025 [16]).
- [Assessment] The WZ-9 drone appears semi-operational, and the 2024 airshow featured a twin-engine "Scorpion B" with reported 8,500 km range (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]).
- [Speculative] The Sept 2025 parade showed a GJ-11 stealth UCAV with folding wings, suggesting shipboard use, plus several new large UCAV and collaborative-combat-aircraft designs. Only the GJ-11 has been seen flying (Aviationist 2025 [8]).
- [Assessment] China's advertised AI and swarming claims are largely "aspirational". Current systems "generally still require human operators" to preprogram targets or pilot them (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]).
- [Assessment] A Mitchell Institute analyst estimates 200+ J-6 fighters converted to drones (J-6W) are based near the Strait, and more than 500 converted overall. They would be used "more like cruise missiles" to overwhelm air defenses in an opening wave, and a Taiwanese official agreed their purpose is to exhaust defenses (Taipei Times/Reuters Mar 2026 [20]).
- [Fact] China's Type 076 assault ship has an electromagnetic catapult and "likely will carry UAVs". Fujian's planned air wing includes "various UAVs" (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]). China also revealed the CM-502X loitering munition (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]).

### E2. Taiwan
- [Fact] The MND tendered for 48,750 domestically made drones (11,270 in 2026 and 37,480 in 2027, mostly small multirotors) under strict bans on Chinese-origin parts (Defense Post Jul 2025 [42]).
- [Contested] Funding for the drone tender is uncertain. The legislature rejected the Cabinet's NT$210B special drone budget and instead enacted (Aug 27, promulgated Sept 11, 2026) a NT$240B, six-year drone-industry act funded through annual budgets, in principle NT$40B a year (Focus Taiwan Sep 2026 [43]).
- [Contested] The "non-red" supply chain: officials say most drone exports are non-red, but industry insiders doubt it. Taiwanese drones can cost up to 3x Chinese equivalents. Output targets rose from 15,000/month by 2028 to 100,000/month by 2030, and exports surged (181,159 units in Jan–Apr 2026, mostly to Europe) (Taipei Times/AFP May 2026 [44]).
- [Fact] Four MQ-9B SkyGuardians were bought for maritime surveillance. The first batch arrived in June 2026 and the rest are due in 2027. A Taiwanese expert says four "would only meet basic operational requirements" (Taipei Times Jun 2026 [47]).
- [Fact] The US approved 720 Switchblade 300s (30 km range) and up to 291 ALTIUS-600M loitering munitions (440 km range, 4 hours endurance), ~$360M, on June 18, 2024 (Arms Control Today 2024 [48]).
- [Assessment] Taiwan had fielded no drone boats as of mid-2025. NCSIST's Kuai Chi attack USV and CSBC's Endeavor Manta are in development and testing (TWZ May 2025 [45]).

### E3. United States / Japan
- [Fact] INDOPACOM's Adm. Paparo described a "hellscape" of thousands of uncrewed surface, undersea and aerial systems to make an invasion force's "lives utterly miserable for a month" while other forces mobilise. He conceded drones alone cannot match China's buildup (Taipei Times 2024 [49]).
- [Assessment] Replicator (announced Aug 2023) aimed for "thousands" of attritable systems by Aug 2025 but had fielded only hundreds. Named systems include Switchblade 600, Altius-600 and Ghost-X, and CRS flagged doubts about Indo-Pacific range and adverse-weather performance (CRS 2025 [50]).
- [Fact] A July 2025 memo reclassified small drones as consumables (like munitions) and set a goal of drones in every squad by end-2026, prioritising Indo-Pacific units. Analysts note small drones' short range limits their relevance across Pacific distances (TWZ 2025 [51]).

### E4. Uncrewed surface and undersea vessels
- [Assessment] Ukrainian sea drones (Magura V5 with ~300 kg warhead in groups of 3–5; Sea Baby ~800 kg) reportedly sank or disabled about a third of Russia's Black Sea Fleet and pushed it to a more distant port. Russia responded with guns, barriers and GPS/satellite jamming (IEEE Spectrum [46]). Drone boats have also downed helicopters (TWZ 2025 [45]).
- [Assessment] In a Taiwan scenario, operator links would likely be jammed, so USVs need local line-of-sight networks or autonomy (IEEE Spectrum [46]).
- [Fact] Replicator reportedly includes the Dive-LD uncrewed undersea vehicle (CRS 2025 [50]). Chinese researchers claimed a submarine-launched sea-air drone ("Feyi") in 2025 (DoD CMPR 2025 [2]).

### E5. Lessons analysts carry from Ukraine and the Red Sea to Taiwan
- [Assessment] Cost asymmetry is central: multimillion-dollar interceptors against tens-of-thousands-dollar drones (Defense News 2026 [33]), with Red Sea interceptor spending near $1B in six months (Stars and Stripes 2024 [31]).
- [Assessment] Drones are consumed at enormous rates, ~10,000 a month in Ukraine in 2023 (RUSI 2023 [35]). Production scale matters: Ukrainian firms reportedly make ~200,000 drones a month (TWZ 2025 [51]).
- [Assessment] Cheap counters (EW, guns, interceptor drones) handle most low-end drones, while scarce missiles are saved for fast or ballistic threats (CSIS 2025 [5]; Defense News 2026 [33]).
- [Speculative] Whether small, short-range drones matter across a ~160 km strait is debated. Longer-range and autonomous types are needed beyond coastal defense (IEEE Spectrum [46]; CRS 2025 [50]).

## Design implications (abstract mechanics)

1. **Bombers are missile trucks, not overflyers.** Bomber units should launch standoff cruise, anti-ship or ballistic missiles from outside SAM range. They should be killable mainly by long-range fighters or by striking them on the ground. (B6)
2. **Fix the carrier roster.** Carriers launch fighters (incl. stealth fighters), AEW, EW jets, ASW helicopters and drones, not heavy bombers. Make the stealth bomber a rare, late-game, land-based "prototype" unit, flagged as projected. (B1, B4, B6)
3. **Gunships arrive with the fleet.** Attack and assault helicopters need ships or seized ground, so tie them to amphibious-ship units or a beachhead event rather than cross-strait raids from turn 1. (B7)
4. **Opening missile salvo.** The first turns should feature ballistic and cruise salvos against airbases, radars and SAM sites, using enemy stock counts by category (short-, medium-, intermediate-range, cruise, hypersonic). (D1)
5. **Planes die on the ground.** Most aircraft losses come from strikes on parked aircraft. Shelters (expensive) and dispersal (fewer sorties) cut losses, and runway hits ground squadrons until repair crews fix them over several turns. (B8)
6. **AEW is a force multiplier and a prime target.** Enemy AEW extends enemy detection and should be hunted by long-range interceptors. Player AEW is scarce (about five aircraft). (B4)
7. **Tankers gate reach.** Near-strait enemy fighters need no tankers, but operations east of the island or long loiters do. Killing or grounding tankers shrinks the enemy's operating radius. (B5)
8. **Finite, expensive magazines.** SAM interceptors are a national stockpile with slow resupply (e.g., ~96 domestic long-range missiles a year; allied resupply delayed years). (D2, D3)
9. **Cost-exchange economy.** Firing a premium interceptor at a cheap drone should feel ruinous (~85:1). Give cheap counters (guns, EW, interceptor drones) an efficiency bonus against slow drones only. (D3, D6)
10. **Saturation and decoys.** Raids mix ~40% decoys with real strike drones, and defenses can't tell them apart until engaged. Leak-through rises sharply in very large raids (>100–150 per turn). (D7, D3)
11. **Speed tiers for interception.** Drones and subsonic cruise missiles are easiest to stop, ballistic missiles harder, and glide vehicles hardest; only top-tier SAMs can try. (D1, D3)
12. **Lasers as point defense.** Cheap per shot with deep magazines, but short-range, one target at a time, and degraded or disabled by rain, haze, cloud or dust. Treat the tech as early-maturity with reliability risk. (D6)
13. **EW is the main counter-drone tool, with fratricide.** Jammer zones degrade all drones in radius, including the player's own unless a C2/integration upgrade is researched. (D6)
14. **Expendable old-fighter drones as cruise-missile stand-ins.** An enemy wave of converted obsolete jets: fast, cheap and numerous, used early to drain SAMs; small interceptor drones are poor against them. (E1)
15. **ISR drones enable enemy targeting.** Long-endurance enemy drones raise enemy accuracy and are easy kills for fighters in wartime. Shooting them down "blinds" a sector for some turns. (E1)
16. **Sea-drone denial.** Cheap USV swarms can push large ships away from the coast. Counters are guns, helicopters, barriers and jamming, and USVs need an autonomy upgrade to work under jamming. (E4)
17. **Allied help is late and limited.** Ally reinforcements and resupply arrive on a delayed, uncertain schedule because of global interceptor depletion, arms backlogs and years-late fighter deliveries. (B2, D2, D3)
18. **Domestic politics shape the budget.** Random budget events (e.g., a legislature cutting a special budget or capping drone spending) can block or delay roster items. (D2, E2)
