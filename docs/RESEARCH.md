# Research: the Taiwan Strait as a design reference

*Isles of Resistance* is fiction. The Republic of Tessera, the Celestine Commonwealth and the Hesperian Federation are invented, and the game is not a forecast of a real war. Its setting borrows the shape of a real situation, though: an island democracy at the center of the world's chip supply, a rising power across a narrow strait, and a distant guarantor. This research makes the game's forces, escalation and pressures credible rather than generic.

## Scope and boundaries
- **Strategic, organizational and capability level only.** The research describes roles, broad capabilities, inventories, constraints and the alternative paths analysts discuss. It contains no attack sequences, targeting guidance, real facilities as targets, exploitable weaknesses or deployment advice, and the game abstracts further still.
- **Sources:** government reports (US DoD China reports 2024–2025, CRS, GAO, ODNI, Japan MOD Joint Staff, Taiwan MND/NSB via reporting), research institutions (CSIS, RAND, CNAS, Hudson, Stimson, Mitchell Institute, US Naval War College CMSI, Jamestown, ASPI, RUSI, IISS via citations) and reputable reporting. About 190 numbered sources across the three briefs, mostly 2023–2026.
- **Every claim carries one label:**
  - **[Fact]**: officially documented or widely confirmed.
  - **[Assessment]**: an expert or official estimate.
  - **[Contested]**: sources disagree; the brief says how.
  - **[Speculative]**: a projection, scenario or wargame assumption.
- **Research date:** November 2026. Events after that are not covered, and many figures (inventories, budgets, delivery dates) change quickly.

## What the research says, in brief
These are the findings that shaped the game. Labels and sources are in the briefs.

1. **Pressure comes in phases, and they don't have to escalate in order.** Analysts describe gray-zone pressure, punitive named exercises, a law-enforcement "quarantine", a military blockade, missile strikes and an amphibious invasion as alternative pathways. A crisis can stall, step back or jump. The US DoD lists four PRC options of rising risk. *[Assessment]*
2. **Warning is short and ambiguous.** Recent drills were announced hours ahead, some operations were never announced, and officials warn a blockade could start "within hours". Exercises look like mobilization, so intelligence comes with confidence levels, not certainty. *[Assessment]*
3. **The coast guard, not the navy, may lead.** A quarantine framed as "customs inspection" stays below the threshold of war and forces dilemmas on the island: escort, protest or comply. Coast guard ship counts rose as air sorties fell in 2026. *[Fact/Assessment]*
4. **Energy is the binding constraint in a blockade.** Taiwan imports about 96% of its energy. LNG reserves cover roughly 11–12 days, and CSIS's blockade games ran out of gas in about ten days, while food was not the limit. *[Fact/Assessment]*
5. **Undersea cables carry nearly all communications, and are cut deniably.** Several incidents in 2023–2025 involved Chinese-linked ships. Backups are low-capacity, and attribution is slow and contested. *[Fact]*
6. **Missiles open the war, and planes die on the ground.** Wargames find about 90% of allied aircraft losses happen on airfields, from ballistic and cruise missile strikes on bases, radars and air defenses. Shelters, dispersal and runway repair decide how fast air power recovers. *[Assessment]*
7. **Bombers are missile trucks.** H-6-class bombers launch cruise and anti-ship missiles from beyond air-defense range rather than overflying targets, and analysts rank them among the hardest assets to counter. *[Assessment]*
8. **Enablers multiply everything.** Airborne early warning extends the attacker's picture, EW aircraft jam radars and data links, and tankers gate reach. They are few, high-value and priority targets. *[Assessment]*
9. **Interceptors are scarce and expensive; drones and decoys are cheap.** Patriot-class rounds cost millions against tens of thousands for attack drones. Decoys made up about 40% of Russian drone launches in late 2025, and stocks take years to rebuild. Cheap counters (guns, EW, interceptor drones, lasers) should handle drones, saving missiles for fast threats. *[Assessment/Contested]*
10. **EW is the main counter-drone tool, and lasers are real but limited.** Directed-energy systems are cheap per shot but short-ranged and degraded by rain, fog and cloud. Israel's Iron Beam entered service in 2025 with exactly that caveat. *[Fact/Assessment]*
11. **Amphibious lift is the invader's center of gravity, and its weakness.** Dedicated lift lands about a division per trip. Civilian ferries and new landing barges add capacity, but only with ports or piers, and experts disagree on how much. In CSIS's games, mobile shore-based anti-ship missiles and submarines sank much of the landing fleet. *[Assessment/Contested]*
12. **Carriers are a sideshow near the strait.** Land-based air power and missiles dominate at this range. Carriers add early warning, fighters and drones on the flanks, and they are high-value targets for both sides. *[Assessment]*
13. **Politics constrains everyone.** Taiwan's divided legislature cut its special defense budget and delayed the general budget a record 266 days. The US paused arms packages around summits, and Japan's public is split on a military response. Allied help is uncertain, late and finite. *[Fact/Contested]*
14. **The island is not just territory.** Strong and stable Taiwanese identity, majority willingness to resist, whole-of-society resilience drills, and lively internal disagreement are all part of the picture. *[Fact]*

## From research to the game
| Research phase (strategy brief) | Game escalation level | What changes in play |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Long Squeeze; P7 Cold Peace | **0 Gray Zone** | Light probing raids, coast guard and militia fleets; the enemy avoids civilian targets |
| P2 Punitive exercise; P3 Customs cordon | **1 Quarantine** (campaign start) | Raids on military sites; coast guard "inspections" and cable cuts as dilemmas |
| P5 Iron Ring | **2 Blockade** | Shipping thins out; the energy reserve starts draining; infrastructure becomes a target |
| P6 Firestorm | **3 Strikes** | Ballistic missile salvos against bases, radars and air defenses; runway damage grounds squadrons |
| P6 Landing | **4 Assault** | Landing groups cross the strait on a clock; attack helicopters fly from them |

Escalation in the game rises with time, enemy losses and the player's own escalatory choices, and falls with restraint and negotiation. It can step back down, and not every campaign reaches an assault.

## Limits
- Open sources lag events, many inventories are estimates, and several key numbers are contested (J-20 counts, interceptor stocks, ferry lift). The game uses them for proportions and relationships, not exact values.
- The research is about plausibility, not prediction. Wargame results depend on their assumptions, and the briefs say so.
- Real systems are named only in the research. The game's forces are fictional composites.
