# Isles of Resistance: World Bible

> *"Everyone wants to decide what Tessera is. We would like a turn."*
> President Imara Solenne, address to the Assembly of Tiles

This is the canonical reference for the game's setting, factions, characters and tone. All in-game text (briefings, directives, broadcasts, intercepts, intel files and epilogues) lives in `Scripts/core/Lore.gd` and should stay consistent with this document.

---

## 1. Premise

A neon-lit, near-future ocean. The old order is under strain. The **Hesperian Federation** has kept the peace (and the shipping lanes, and its own pre-eminence) for eighty years. Across the strait, the **Celestine Commonwealth** has climbed out of a century of humiliation into the world's largest industrial economy, and it wants the recognition that it believes should come with that.

Between them sits the **Tessera Archipelago**: a crowded, argumentative, multilingual island democracy that manufactures nearly every advanced microchip on Earth. Tessera is not a prize to be won. It is a country, and it has opinions.

The game begins when the Commonwealth declares a **Peace Enforcement Quarantine** over Tesseran airspace. The player commands Tessera's **Air Defense Command**. Over a campaign of escalating waves, the player decides how to defend the islands. The player also decides whose help to take, at what price, and what kind of country is left when the sirens stop.

### The central tension
- An **established power** trying to preserve the order that made it rich, safe and central. It is sincere about some of its principles and selective about others.
- A **rising power** demanding the respect and the sphere its size seems to warrant. Its grievances are genuine, and its methods are coercive.
- An **island** whose prosperity is the reason both of them care, and possibly the reason it will be destroyed.
- **Everyone else** (regional states, corporations, hackers, insurers, fishmongers) trying to survive the weather.

## 2. Geography

- **The Tessera Archipelago.** Dozens of islands with forested ridges, terraced hills and neon port cities. The capital is **Lumen**. The semiconductor city of **Silica Point** never sleeps. Other cities include the old fishing towns of **Saltmere** and **Kestrel Bay**, and the customs-era port of **Old Customs**.
- **The Lantern Strait.** The water between Tessera and the Celestine coast. The **Lantern Line**, an informal median line, was respected for decades and is now crossed daily.
- **The Celestine Commonwealth** lies east, beyond the strait. Its carrier groups hold station in the deep water at the eastern edge of the map.
- **The Western Ocean** lies behind the islands. **Hesperia** is far beyond it, and so is Task Force Palisade, which is officially "over the horizon".
- **The Littoral Compact** states curve along the southern sea lanes. The **Kingdom of Varesk** lies to the north.

## 3. A short history (as each side tells it)

| Era | Celestine account | Tesseran account | Hesperian account |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Charter Century** (150 to 85 years ago) | Foreign powers forced "unequal charters" on our coast and stole our ports, Tessera among them. | Our grandparents' grandparents were here first: the Shoal Peoples, then traders, then refugees. The charter was imposed on *us* too. | Regrettable. Long ago. Different administration. |
| **The Long War** and **Pax Hesperia** (85 years ago) | The Federation replaced the old empires with a new one and called it "order". | Hesperian fleets kept the war from our shores, and later kept a dictatorship in power here. Both things are true. | We built the institutions that ended the age of empires. You're welcome. |
| **The Harbor Authority years** | Tessera was run by a foreign-backed junta. | Thirty years of martial law under the Harbor Authority. | A stable partner in a difficult region. |
| **The Lantern Spring** (40 years ago) | A color revolution. | A million paper lanterns on the Lumen waterfront, and then elections. | A triumph of democracy (we were briefly unsure). |
| **The Great Renewal** | Eight hundred million people lifted out of poverty in a generation. | Our biggest trading partner, and the reason our foreign ministry has insomnia. | A peer competitor. |
| **The Clean Compute Act** (10 years ago) | Technological containment, an economic blockade in a lab coat. | Every chip we sell now needs three governments' permission. | Responsible export controls. |
| **The Gray Hours** (the last 2 years) | Routine patrols of our own waters. | Daily incursions, "unexplained" undersea cable faults, coast guard standoffs. | Concerning. |
| **The Saltmere Ferry Incident** | A Tesseran ferry rammed a Commonwealth coast guard cutter. Nine sailors drowned. | The cutter cut across the ferry's bow in the dark. Fourteen passengers drowned. | Both sides urged to show restraint. |

Three days after the Saltmere Ferry Incident, the Steering Council declared the **Quarantine**. Nobody agrees on what happened that night. The game's Intel Archive holds three versions, and one of them comes from a leaked bridge recording that satisfies no one.

## 4. Factions

Every faction has legitimate interests, real grievances, internal disagreements and contradictions it would rather not discuss.

### The Republic of Tessera
**Symbol:** a lantern over a mosaic tile. **Colors:** teal and gold. **Motto:** *"Every tile its own light."*

*Wants:* to keep governing itself, keep its economy and keep its people alive, without becoming either superpower's forward base.

*Grievance:* everyone talks about Tessera, and almost no one talks *to* it.

*Internal factions:*
- **The Lantern Movement.** Young, digital-native and sovereignty-minded. They distrust the Commonwealth and also distrust Hesperia's motives. Some want formal independence; most want to stop being asked.
- **The Harbor Party.** Accommodationists, with their base in the trading towns and among older voters. Some feel genuine cultural kinship with the mainland. Most simply believe that war is the worst outcome on any spreadsheet. They are called traitors in the morning and realists by lunch.
- **Tile & Wafer.** The business and technocrat bloc around the semiconductor industry. They want stability, contracts and above all no explosions near the cleanrooms.

*Contradiction:* the chip industry is both Tessera's shield and its target. Tessera wants Hesperian protection without Hesperian control, and Celestine trade without Celestine rule. On a good day it gets both, and on a bad day it gets neither.

### The Celestine Commonwealth
**Symbol:** a nine-pointed rising star over a wave. **Colors:** crimson and ivory. **Governing body:** the **Steering Council**, chaired by the First Steward.

*Wants:* the **Recompletion** (reunification with what it calls "the Island Province"), an end to containment, technological self-reliance, and recognition as a co-author of the world's rules rather than a guest at someone else's table.

*Grievance:* the Charter Century was real. So were the sanctions, the export controls and a world order designed before the Commonwealth had a seat.

*Internal factions:*
- **Restorationists.** Military and nationalist. They believe the strategic window is closing as Hesperia rearms.
- **Pragmatists.** The trade and finance ministries and the coastal provinces, where eleven million jobs depend on Tesseran-owned factories. They want a face-saving deal.
- **Technocrats.** They believe time is on the Commonwealth's side and that this war is premature.

*Contradiction:* the Commonwealth calls the operation a *quarantine* to keep it below the threshold of a "war". It insists the islanders are compatriots awaiting liberation, then bombs their radars. It needs Tesseran chips, and it is shooting at the factory.

### The Hesperian Federation
**Symbol:** a compass rose over a setting sun. **Colors:** navy and white. **Order:** **Pax Hesperia**, underwritten by the **Hesper** reserve currency and the Federation's fleets.

*Wants:* to preserve its alliances, the sea lanes and the credibility of its commitments, and to keep the world's most important chips somewhere friendly. Ideally that means Hesperia.

*Grievance:* it built the order that everyone is now prospering under, and it is tired of paying for it.

*Internal factions:*
- **Primacy hawks.** They hold that credibility is indivisible: lose Tessera and you lose the region.
- **Restrainers.** "Not one more Hesper for somebody else's chip factory."
- **The Market Caucus.** Contractors, cloud providers and banks, who are invested in tension and in calm, in that order.

*Doctrine:* the **Ambiguity Doctrine**. The Federation never says whether it would fight for Tessera. This has kept the peace for forty years, and it is keeping Tessera awake tonight.

*Contradiction:* it champions sovereignty and self-determination while pressing Tessera to relocate its crown-jewel factories under the **Clean Compute Act**.

### The Littoral Compact
**Symbol:** a ring of interlocking hulls. **Colors:** sea green.

A bloc of southern maritime states. They trade with the Commonwealth, run exercises with the Federation and insure everybody. They want the shipping lanes open, no war and no requirement to choose. Choosing gets demanded of them daily.

### The Kingdom of Varesk
A northern island kingdom that hosts Hesperian airbases. It remembers being occupied, it is rearming quickly, and it is privately terrified of being next. It appears mostly in news reports, and once in a directive about basing rights for Hesperian tankers.

### Corporations and non-state actors
- **Tessera Lithographic Works (TLW), "the Foundry".** Makes the world's most advanced chips at **Fab 18** in Silica Point. Its CEO preaches the **Glass Shield**, the theory that no one bombs the place the whole world depends on. TLW is also quietly building fabs in Hesperia, which slowly weakens the shield.
- **Harmony Grid.** The Commonwealth's state-champion telecom and AI conglomerate. It offers "Free 7G for the Island Province", and its Harmony Score civic ratings come pre-installed.
- **Halberd Dynamics.** A Hesperian defense prime that sells the **Longbow** SAM and the **Lumen** laser, whose firing key is a subscription. Its CEO's motto is "peace through superior quarterly earnings".
- **Brightline.** The Hesperian cloud giant that hosts half of Tessera's government, under a terms of service nobody has read.
- **Null Collective.** Hacktivists who leak everyone's secrets equally and communicate in commit messages. Their spokes-handle is **segfault**. Their motto is "We don't pick sides. Sides are a data structure with too many null references."
- **Meridian Mutual.** A maritime insurer. Its war-risk premium for the Lantern Strait is currently listed as "lol".

### Media
- **CCB, Celestine Concord Broadcasting.** Calm, official, euphemistic. Separatists are always "a small minority".
- **HNN, the Hesperian News Network.** Breathless, partisan, ad-supported. Every headline ends with what it means for markets.
- **LPB, Lumen Public Broadcasting.** Earnest and tired.
- **Strait Talk.** A pirate call-in radio show broadcast from the back of a Saltmere fish market by **Auntie Sol**. It is the voice of everyone nobody asked.

## 5. People

Characters are not mouthpieces for their nations. Each has a job, a fear and a blind spot.

**Tessera**
- **President Imara Solenne.** A former TLW process engineer who became president of a fractious coalition. She is trying to keep every option open long enough to survive. *"Everyone wants to decide what Tessera is. We would like a turn."*
- **Captain Teo Ardent.** Air Defense Command, the player's briefing officer. He is dry, competent and running on vending-machine coffee. *"Rule one: the radar is never wrong. Rule two: check it anyway."*
- **Dr. Ines Haldane.** CEO of TLW. She believes in the Glass Shield and is building its replacement abroad, just in case. *"We are not a bargaining chip. We make the chips. The difference is about four nanometers."*
- **Mara Okafor-Lin.** Night-shift process engineer on Fab 18, Line 3. *"The steppers don't stop for air raids. If they cool down it's four months to recalibrate. So we stay. Somebody has to explain that to my mother."*
- **Auntie Sol.** A Saltmere fishmonger and the host of Strait Talk. *"Nobody asked me which superpower I prefer. I prefer my roof."*
- **Kai Revers.** A Lantern Movement organizer. *"We didn't light lanterns forty years ago so somebody else could hold the match."*
- **Deputy Speaker Hollis Marrane.** Leader of the Harbor Party. *"I am called a traitor in the morning and a realist by lunch. The war doesn't care which."*

**Celestine Commonwealth**
- **First Steward Taveth Morai.** Chair of the Steering Council. His legacy depends on the Recompletion, and his economy is slowing. *"History is patient. The Council is patient. The fiscal year is not."*
- **Marshal Idrena Kost.** Eastern Theater Command, and the leading Restorationist. *"Every year we wait, they pour another runway. The door is open now."*
- **Deputy Minister Sel Varo.** Commerce, a Pragmatist and a back-channel contact. He studied at Lumen University. *"Explain to me how bombing our best customers is a growth strategy."*
- **Flight Lieutenant Ilan Sorei.** A Monsoon bomber pilot from the coastal province of Haveth. His sister works at a Tesseran-owned plant. *"They told us the islanders were waiting to be liberated. The islanders keep shooting at me. One of these is a communication problem."*
- **Director Lume Haskar.** Head of Harmony Grid. *"We connect 2.1 billion devices. We'd be delighted to make it 2.1 billion and eleven."*

**Hesperian Federation**
- **President Corwin Ashdale.** It is an election year. *"Our commitment to Tessera is ironclad, and also under review."*
- **Admiral Constance Whitlow.** Task Force Palisade. *"My orders are to be visible but not present. I'm told that's a strategy."*
- **Under-Secretary Delphine Marchetti.** Commerce, and the author of the Clean Compute Act. *"Resilience means the chips are made somewhere we can protect. Ideally somewhere with our postal codes."*
- **Senator Rook Halvorsen.** A restrainer. *"Not one more Hesper for somebody else's chip factory. Unless the factory is in my state."*
- **Brannock Pryce.** CEO of Halberd Dynamics. *"Peace through superior quarterly earnings."*

**Elsewhere**
- **Secretary Rasa Kemalu.** Littoral Compact. *"We trade with the Commonwealth, drill with the Federation and insure everyone. Neutrality is a full-time job."*
- **segfault.** Null Collective. Speaks in lowercase commit messages.

## 7. Terms and symbols

- **The Glass Shield.** The belief that chip dependence protects Tessera from attack.
- **Recompletion.** The Celestine term for reunification ("completing the map").
- **The Quarantine.** The Commonwealth's name for the air campaign. Tesserans call it **the Siege**, and HNN calls it **the Lantern Strait Crisis**.
- **The Ambiguity Doctrine.** Hesperia's policy of never saying what it would do.
- **The Clean Compute Act.** Hesperian export controls and friend-shoring.
- **Harmony Score.** The Celestine civic rating that comes free with Harmony Grid.
- **The Lantern Line.** The median line of the strait.
- **Hesper / Tessar.** Currencies.

## 8. How the story plays

- **Acts.** *I · Quarantine*, *II · The Glass Shield*, *III · The Long Night* and *Finale · Terms*. Acts are spread across the campaign's waves at every difficulty.
- **Directives.** Political dilemmas from ministers, admirals, CEOs, hackers and back channels, with concrete trade-offs. Examples: accept Hesperian interceptors in exchange for moving a fab line; take a Harmony Grid "gift"; leak or keep a back-channel offer; draft fab engineers into air defense. Choices change supplies, research, enemy wave strength, what the enemy knows and what the ending says.
- **Public Resolve** (domestic morale) changes income. **Hesperian Commitment** brings arms shipments after each wave is repelled.
- **The Foundry and the Glass Shield.** Silica Point is spared by Commonwealth planners while the Glass Shield holds. Some choices crack it, and then the Foundry becomes a prime target.
- **Cyber operations.** Spend research on hacks such as *Null Pointer*, *Rubber Duck*, *Merge Conflict* and *Rollback*.
- **Broadcasts.** A ticker carries CCB, HNN, LPB, Strait Talk and advertising. Each outlet tells the same events its own way.
- **Intercepts.** Commonwealth pilots and controllers talk during the enemy phase. They are tired, funny, scared and loyal.
- **Intel Archive.** Dossiers, documents and contradictory accounts that unlock as the story unfolds.
- **Epilogues.** Depend on how the war ended and what was traded to end it, and close with codas from ordinary people.

## 9. Tone rules

1. **No monoliths.** Every population contains hawks, doves, opportunists, the exhausted and the indifferent. Commonwealth pilots are people. Hesperian officials are not saviors, and Tesserans disagree with each other loudly.
2. **Recognizable, not literal.** Draw on real themes (contested sovereignty, maritime chokepoints, chip supply chains, export controls, deterrence, cyber conflict, information war), but never map a real person, party or event one-to-one.
3. **Tessera has agency.** It makes choices, pays prices and argues about both.
4. **Satire aims up.** Mock institutions, euphemisms, PR language and corporate absurdity, not ordinary people and not suffering.
5. **The humor is engineering humor.** Firmware betas, deploys on Fridays, terms of service, tickets marked WONTFIX, *works on my machine*. The joke is always that complex systems are run by tired people.
6. **Gameplay first.** Text is short, skippable and readable mid-battle. Lore deepens the game and never blocks it.
